# On-device hand cues

Gesture input is optional. Offer and Accept buttons use the same server commands and remain available throughout. MediaPipe locates hand landmarks; PORTAL STAGE applies its own deliberate intent gate. It does not recognize a physical umbrella, infer whether one is open, identify a person, segment a body, or validate real-world occlusion.

`useGestureInput(video, options)` lives in `src/vision/use-gesture-input.ts`. Only its explicit `enable()` action starts the worker and loads assets. It consumes an existing private camera preview and never requests a camera itself. `disable()` and unmount terminate the worker and timers. Unsupported workers, failed assets, slow inference, an inactive tab, or a missing feed fall back to buttons with visible status.

A successful inference with no eligible selected hand reports “Tracking active — no selected hand detected,” rather than a runtime pause. This is expected for the synthetic transport plate and does not establish a successful human cue. Weak signal and a calibrated-hand mismatch show separate lighting/framing or hand-selection guidance. Worker, decoder and inference-age failures retain their distinct fallback messages; all paths keep button parity.

The bundled `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.2.0 runtime, SIMD/non-SIMD WebAssembly, and fixed `hand_landmarker/float16/1` model are served from `/vision`. No runtime CDN is required. Frames are resized to at most 640 px on their longest edge and processed at a maximum of approximately 10 inferences per second with only one frame in flight. The worker closes each transferred bitmap; detection data is transient. There is no recording, raw-frame upload, landmark persistence, identity template, or local-storage calibration in this implementation. Semantic Offer/Accept requests use the show's normal action API. Ongoing normalized positions use the private LiveKit room's disposable data path through `media.sendAnchor`, with immediate local presentation, bounded latest-only sending and an expiring calibration fallback. They do not write a whole show document for each movement.

The upstream SDK includes an aggregate usage-metrics sender. This isolated worker permits only same-origin fetch/XHR and blocks its external endpoint. That implemented worker guard provides the network restriction; the app currently has security headers and no configured Content Security Policy. Model input stays local; LiveKit transport of a performer camera is a separate, explicitly enabled media action.

## Calibration and intent

Confirm framing, the intended hand, and the cue region before assigning a `GestureCalibration`. All rectangles and points are normalized to 0–1. `apertureCalibration({slot,handedness,videoWidth,videoHeight})` derives the actual centered cover crop from the video's intrinsic dimensions and the shared 650 × 562 aperture. It returns null until dimensions are known; manual marker placement and durable calibration remain available without a camera. `cameraToStage()` rejects cropped-out, unsafe and nonfinite points, maps them once to visible aperture coordinates, and smooths a stable anchor with an 0.018 dead zone. A lost hand cancels dwell. The gesture engine retains its last stable point internally; the public disposable position expires to durable calibration after 1500 ms without fresh eligible samples.

Program uses an unmirrored camera. The private preview uses the same 650:562 aspect and centered cover crop, followed by a CSS mirror. New aperture calibrations therefore use `mirrored:false` for raw landmarks. The manual preview marker is already in visible aperture coordinates: invert its x coordinate once to save the unmirrored Program position. `rawRectToMirroredAperture()` applies the crop and inverse mirror to draw a raw cue region in that preview. Confirm direction visually on each actual camera, especially virtual cameras that may already flip pixels.

The supported visible grip region is x=0.16–0.84, y=0.30–0.72. Calibration takes the corresponding subsection of the intrinsic camera crop and maps it to that same visible region; it does not stretch the full video or full crop into a smaller safe rectangle. Cue regions are x=0.66–0.82 for A or x=0.18–0.34 for B, y=0.32–0.70, in visible Program coordinates. These are converted back to raw camera rectangles for detection and back to visible coordinates for cue inset/drift gates. A changed intrinsic aspect pauses gesture cues and position output until a fresh calibration confirmation; a same-aspect resolution change keeps the mapping valid. The older `defaultCalibration()` remains a legacy helper rather than the camera-geometry setup path.

Automated pixel-space geometry checks verify this transform for wide, portrait/narrow, square and matching-aspect inputs. They do not prove physical hand-to-grip registration on a real camera. That human test, including the authored grip, framing, smoothing, lighting and motion, remains pending. No physical hand-alignment result is claimed.

Each cue requires all of these conditions:

- Explicitly armed input and a confirmed valid region.
- The chosen hand, finite in-frame landmarks, and signal score at least 0.8. The model's detection, hand-presence, and tracking thresholds are also 0.8. The visible signal score combines handedness classification and available landmark visibility; it is **not** a calibrated probability of intent or measured gesture accuracy.
- A geometrically open palm inside the inset cue region with bounded drift. The engine requires a neutral/lowered hand after arming or an offer change, avoiding acceptance from a hand already raised for an earlier cue.
- Continuous dwell of 900 ms to Offer or 1100 ms to Accept. A frame gap over 240 ms, lost signal, or an out-of-region hand cancels progress. A result over 400 ms old cannot trigger a cue.
- For Offer, the performer owns the prop and is eligible to offer. For Accept, the performer is the expected receiver of a current offer ID. A new offer cannot inherit the old offer's dwell.
- A 1800 ms cooldown and one request per eligibility/arming cycle. The server still checks ownership, version, epoch, consent, admission, and expiry before committing anything.

Two ambiguous detections of the selected hand are rejected. These conservative gates may miss intentional gestures. Re-arm or use the matching button; a failed network command is not treated as a successful transfer. Framing confirmation is a user-defined geometric setup, not a measured guarantee of hand-to-prop alignment.

## Observed and pending verification

Automated tests compare the shared crop transform with independent pixel-space CSS-cover math, including 16:9 and portrait/narrow sources, safe-region edges, mirrored overlays, narrow preview widths, manual-marker parity, aspect changes and identical visible cue thresholds. Gesture tests cover dead zones, disarmed input, wrong ownership/receiver, current offer IDs, continuous dwell, confidence and handedness loss, stale frames, replacement offers, ambiguous hands, held-palm repeat prevention, and bundled asset integrity. Disposable-position tests also cover immutable peer metadata/identity, show/epoch, bounds, age/order/rate/size, backpressure, reset and fallback. They use synthetic observations and SDK mocks; they do not establish real-user accuracy or physical rendered grip alignment.

Actual worker initialization and camera-to-landmark timing require browser verification. Intent success and false-activation rates remain pending trials with consenting people across varied skin tones, lighting, sleeves, occlusion, portrait/landscape cameras, one/two hands, seated positions, mobility needs, and CPU load. Every trial must retain button parity. Do not quote the plan's accuracy or latency targets as measured outcomes.
